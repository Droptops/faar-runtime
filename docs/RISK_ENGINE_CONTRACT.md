# Risk Engine Contract

## Why this contract exists

Suppose a portfolio has $100 of remaining daily/position capacity. Two workers read the same snapshot and independently authorize $75 trades. Both per-intent decisions are locally valid; jointly they are not.

FAAR prevents the *same trusted risk state* from authorizing two distinct new intents, and refuses a state version older than any version already consumed by an intent or a retry. That mechanism is only sound if the risk engine follows the versioning contract below.

## Required snapshot fields

A signed risk snapshot includes:

```text
scope                       (required)
state_version               (required, >= 1)
observed_at                 (required, timezone-aware)
position_after_usd
daily_turnover_after_usd
daily_loss_usd
market_data_age_seconds
requested_slippage_bps
price_impact_bps
actions_in_window
circuit_breaker_active
data_complete
source_count
sources_agree
```

These are the exact `RiskSnapshot` field names (`faar/models.py`) and the keys of
`schemas/risk-snapshot.schema.json`. `parse_risk` rejects any other key, so a
misspelled field is an error rather than a silently ignored control.

`requested_slippage_bps` and `price_impact_bps` are the signer's claims about the
snapshot; they gate the decision but are not enforced at the venue. The
execution-side bound is the payload's `max_slippage_bps` (or an order's
`limit_price`), which a slippage-capped grant requires and which travels in the
permit-bound request (`ADAPTER_CONTRACT.md` A1, I-39).

## Versioning invariant

For a `(grant_id, version, scope)`:

```text
state_version(N+1)
```

must not be issued until the risk engine's state incorporates all reservations/economic effects that it intends to precede N+1.

The required mental model is closer to a monotonic portfolio ledger than a timestamped quote cache.

### Unsafe implementation

```text
worker A -> risk v10 -> $75 allowed
worker B -> risk v11 -> $75 allowed
```

where v11 was generated by merely incrementing a counter without incorporating A's held reservation. FAAR cannot detect that semantic lie from the version number alone.

### Safe implementation direction

```text
portfolio state v10
      │
      ├─ FAAR claims v10 for intent A
      │
      ▼
risk service observes/reserves A
      │
      ▼
portfolio state v11
      │
      └─ may authorize intent B against updated capacity
```

## Reservation integration

A production system should choose one of:

1. risk engine owns reservation and produces a signed reservation token;
2. FAAR datastore and risk engine share a serializable transaction/ledger;
3. risk engine consumes FAAR's committed/held reservation stream before issuing the next version;
4. lower-level venue contract/account controls enforce the aggregate limit independently.

## Staleness

`observed_at` and market-data age are independent. A fresh signature over an old market snapshot is not fresh risk evidence.

## Failure policy

If version order, source consistency, or snapshot freshness cannot be proven, the correct result is DEFER/STOP—not “use the last known safe value.” A proven limit breach (position, turnover, loss, slippage, price impact, velocity) is a DENY.

## Production gate

A live-money adapter is blocked until this integration contract is implemented and concurrency-tested across multiple processes/nodes.
